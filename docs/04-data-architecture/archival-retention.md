# Archival and Retention

## Orders
- Can it be deleted? No, not as a normal business practice.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Order history should remain available for as long as the business needs service continuity, reporting, and dispute review.
- Why should history be preserved? Order history supports customer service, audit review, financial reconciliation, and business analysis.

## Payments
- Can it be deleted? No, not as a normal business practice.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Payment history should remain available for the full business retention period needed for finance oversight and dispute resolution.
- Why should history be preserved? Payment history supports trust, auditability, and resolution of financial questions.

## Inventory Movements
- Can it be deleted? No, not as a normal business practice.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Inventory movement history should remain available long enough to explain stock changes and support operational review.
- Why should history be preserved? It protects inventory accuracy, explains stock adjustments, and supports investigations.

## Products
- Can it be deleted? No, not as a normal business practice.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Product history should remain available as long as the product has business relevance or appears in historical orders and reports.
- Why should history be preserved? Historical product information preserves catalog continuity and supports product-related reporting.

## Customers
- Can it be deleted? Generally no; inactive customers should be archived rather than removed.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Customer history should remain available long enough to support service continuity and business review.
- Why should history be preserved? Customer history supports relationship understanding, service continuity, and reporting.

## Employees
- Can it be deleted? Generally no; historical employment information should be retained.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Employee history should remain available for governance, accountability, and audit purposes.
- Why should history be preserved? It supports approval clarity, operational accountability, and compliance review.

## Notifications
- Can it be deleted? Not as a general rule; important communication history should be retained.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Notification history should remain available long enough to preserve customer and staff communication context.
- Why should history be preserved? Notification history supports dispute resolution, business communication review, and service continuity.

## AI Logs
- Can it be deleted? No, not as a normal business practice.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? AI logs should remain available for as long as the business needs accountability and review of assisted actions.
- Why should history be preserved? AI history supports traceability, policy review, and operational learning.

## Audit Logs
- Can it be deleted? No.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Audit logs should remain available for the longest relevant business retention period.
- Why should history be preserved? Audit history is essential for accountability, investigation, and governance.

## Production Records
- Can it be deleted? No, not as a normal business practice.
- Can it be archived? Yes.
- How long should history remain? Production records should remain available long enough to support inventory continuity, planning review, and operational history.
- Why should history be preserved? Production history supports understanding of finished goods, material usage, and operational decisions.

## Business value of historical data
Historical data is valuable because it preserves the business memory of the company. It helps explain what happened, supports accountability, improves reporting, strengthens customer service, and reduces the risk of repeating past mistakes. In a growing business, history is not just a record of the past; it becomes a foundation for better decisions in the future.
