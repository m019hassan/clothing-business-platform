# Data Lifecycle

## Products
- Creation: A product enters the business catalog when it is introduced for sale or internal use.
- Updates: Product information may change as pricing, status, or merchandising needs evolve.
- Active usage: The product becomes part of orders, inventory planning, promotions, and reporting.
- Archiving: A product may be retired from active selling while preserving its business history.
- Deletion policy: Products should not be deleted casually; inactive products should be marked as no longer active where appropriate.
- Historical retention: Product history should be preserved to support reporting, order context, and business continuity.

## Inventory
- Creation: Inventory comes into being when stock enters the business through receipt, production, or transfer.
- Updates: Inventory changes as stock is reserved, adjusted, moved, consumed, or returned.
- Active usage: Inventory is actively used to support sales, fulfillment, production, and operational planning.
- Archiving: Inventory records should remain meaningful after use, especially where they support historical review.
- Deletion policy: Inventory should not be removed as a matter of routine; inactive or resolved stock should be retained in a historical or closed state.
- Historical retention: Historical inventory movement should be preserved to maintain trust in availability and explain stock changes.

## Orders
- Creation: An order begins when a customer request is accepted as a business order.
- Updates: Order details may change as the business confirms, adjusts, or resolves the order.
- Active usage: Orders remain active while fulfillment, payment, and customer communication are in progress.
- Archiving: Completed or closed orders should be preserved for history, reporting, and review.
- Deletion policy: Orders should not be deleted casually; they should be closed or archived when no longer active.
- Historical retention: Order history should be retained to support customer service, audit review, and business analysis.

## Payments
- Creation: A payment enters the business process when a financial commitment is initiated for an order.
- Updates: Payment state may change as verification, disputes, refunds, or settlement activity occur.
- Active usage: Payments are active while the business is reviewing, confirming, or resolving the financial transaction.
- Archiving: Completed and resolved payments should remain available for historical review and reporting.
- Deletion policy: Payment records should not be deleted casually; they should be archived or closed after resolution.
- Historical retention: Payment history should be preserved for trust, dispute handling, and finance oversight.

## Customers
- Creation: A customer is created when the business recognizes a customer relationship or account.
- Updates: Customer information may change over time as contact details, preferences, or business status evolve.
- Active usage: Customers remain active while they buy, interact, or receive business communication.
- Archiving: Inactive customers may be retained for historical relationship context and service continuity.
- Deletion policy: Customer records should generally be archived rather than removed unless there is a clear business reason.
- Historical retention: Customer history should be preserved to support service continuity, reporting, and relationship understanding.

## Employees
- Creation: An employee record begins when the business recognizes a staff member’s role in operations.
- Updates: Employee information may change as responsibilities, access, or business status change.
- Active usage: Employees remain active while performing operational or administrative work.
- Archiving: Former employees may be retained in a historical or inactive state for review and audit purposes.
- Deletion policy: Employee records should generally be retained for governance and audit, even after departure.
- Historical retention: Employment history should be preserved to support accountability and operational continuity.

## Notifications
- Creation: A notification is created when a business event requires communication.
- Updates: Notification details may be updated while the message remains in progress or pending review.
- Active usage: Notifications are active while they are relevant to the recipient or the business workflow.
- Archiving: Delivered or resolved notifications should remain accessible for review and historical context.
- Deletion policy: Notifications should generally be retained rather than deleted, especially where they support audit or customer communication history.
- Historical retention: Notification history should be preserved where the business needs to understand communication outcomes.

## AI Commands
- Creation: An AI command is created when a request is submitted for business assistance.
- Updates: AI command context may change as the request is reviewed, clarified, or approved.
- Active usage: AI commands remain active while they are under review or being acted upon.
- Archiving: Completed or closed AI commands should be preserved for traceability and audit.
- Deletion policy: AI command records should generally be retained, especially where they support accountability or compliance review.
- Historical retention: AI command history should be preserved to demonstrate how assisted actions were requested and completed.
