# Backup and Recovery Strategy

## Backup objectives
The business needs backups that preserve essential operational information and support recovery when the primary business system becomes unavailable. Backup planning should focus on protecting the business’s ability to continue serving customers, maintaining inventory confidence, and preserving records for review.

## Business continuity expectations
The primary application will run on a cloud server, while regular backups will be stored on a home server for disaster recovery. This arrangement should support the business in the event of service disruption, accidental loss, or prolonged system unavailability. The goal is not only to preserve data, but also to preserve the business’s ability to recover key operations with minimal confusion.

## Recovery expectations
The business should be able to recover the most important business data and restore operational continuity within a reasonable business timeframe. Recovery expectations should prioritize:
- Customer order and payment information
- Inventory and reservation records
- Product and catalog data
- Approval and audit records
- Critical business files and documents

## Backup frequency
Backups should be taken on a regular schedule that reflects the business’s operational rhythm. The business should maintain both frequent routine backups and longer-term retention of older recovery points so that recent changes can be restored without losing important history.

## Restore expectations
The business should expect that recovery procedures can restore business data to a known and reviewable state. Restore processes should be clear enough that business leaders can understand what is recovered, what is not, and what needs manual verification after restoration.

## Disaster scenarios
The backup strategy should support business recovery in common disruption scenarios such as:
- Temporary service outage of the primary application
- Loss or corruption of important business records
- Accidental deletion or overwriting of business data
- Extended disruption that makes the main system unavailable
- Loss of confidence in current data due to a business process error

## Business priorities during recovery
During recovery, the business should prioritize:
1. Preserving customer trust and service continuity
2. Protecting inventory accuracy and order commitments
3. Restoring financial and approval records
4. Re-establishing access to important documents and files
5. Preserving audit history and business accountability

## Recovery governance
Recovery should be treated as a business event, not only an IT event. Business owners should know what must be restored first, what can wait, and which records must be reviewed before normal operations resume.
