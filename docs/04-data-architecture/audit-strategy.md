# Audit Strategy

## Purpose of auditing
Auditing exists to preserve trust in the business platform. It gives the business a clear record of important actions so that decisions, approvals, and changes can be understood later by managers, owners, finance staff, and operational teams.

## Business value
Auditing supports:
- Accountability for sensitive business actions
- Faster dispute resolution and review
- Confidence in approvals and financial handling
- Better understanding of operational changes over time
- Stronger governance for inventory, payments, permissions, and AI-assisted work

## Audit principles
The business should treat auditing as a standard expectation for significant actions, especially where the outcome affects money, stock, access, or customer trust. Every audited action should be understandable in terms of what changed, why it changed, who performed it, and when it happened.

## Actions that must always be audited

### User login
- What should be recorded: User identity, date and time, success or failure, and the business context of access.
- Why it matters: It supports accountability and helps investigate suspicious or unusual access.
- Who may perform the action: The user, with support from identity administrators when review is needed.
- Retention expectations: Retain for the full business review period required for access and security oversight.

### Permission changes
- What should be recorded: The affected role or permission, the change made, the approver, and the reason for the change.
- Why it matters: Permission changes impact authority and must remain visible to maintain control.
- Who may perform the action: Managers or authorized administrators.
- Retention expectations: Retain for the full governance and audit period.

### Product creation
- What should be recorded: Product identity, the person who created it, the business context of creation, and the initial catalog status.
- Why it matters: It helps the business understand when and why a new offer entered the catalog.
- Who may perform the action: Catalog administrators or authorized product managers.
- Retention expectations: Retain as part of the product history.

### Product updates
- What should be recorded: Which product changed, what changed, who approved or made the change, and the reason where relevant.
- Why it matters: Product changes affect pricing, availability, and customer experience and must remain traceable.
- Who may perform the action: Catalog administrators or managers with appropriate authority.
- Retention expectations: Retain with the related product record and historical product context.

### Inventory adjustments
- What should be recorded: The affected stock, the quantity or value adjusted, the reason for adjustment, the person responsible, and the approval status.
- Why it matters: Inventory adjustments directly affect availability and trust in stock records.
- Who may perform the action: Inventory staff with manager approval for significant adjustments.
- Retention expectations: Retain as part of inventory history and operational review.

### Order status changes
- What should be recorded: The order, the previous state, the new state, the actor, and the reason where relevant.
- Why it matters: Order movement affects customer commitments, fulfillment, and service expectations.
- Who may perform the action: Sales staff, fulfillment staff, or managers depending on the status change.
- Retention expectations: Retain with the order history.

### Payment verification
- What should be recorded: The payment, the verification result, the person who verified it, and any supporting approval context.
- Why it matters: Payment verification affects the business’s financial trust and customer fulfillment confidence.
- Who may perform the action: Authorized finance staff or managers.
- Retention expectations: Retain for financial review and dispute handling.

### Refund approval
- What should be recorded: The refund request, the amount, the decision, the approver, and the reason.
- Why it matters: Refunds affect customer trust and financial accountability.
- Who may perform the action: Authorized finance staff or managers.
- Retention expectations: Retain for financial and customer service review.

### Discount changes
- What should be recorded: The discount, the change made, the effective period, the actor, and the approval context.
- Why it matters: Discount changes affect pricing strategy and customer expectations.
- Who may perform the action: Catalog or pricing administrators with appropriate authority.
- Retention expectations: Retain with the promotion or discount history.

### AI commands
- What should be recorded: The request submitted, the purpose, the requesting user or role, and the approval context if required.
- Why it matters: AI commands represent assisted business actions and must stay visible for governance and review.
- Who may perform the action: Authorized users with the appropriate permission.
- Retention expectations: Retain for operational review and accountability.

### AI actions
- What should be recorded: The action taken, the business outcome, the relevant AI command, and the review status.
- Why it matters: The business must understand what assistance was provided and whether the result was accepted or rejected.
- Who may perform the action: AI systems acting under approved business authority, reviewed by authorized staff.
- Retention expectations: Retain for traceability, governance, and learning.

### Employee management
- What should be recorded: The employee involved, the action taken, the authority behind the action, and any role or status changes.
- Why it matters: Employee management affects responsibility, access, and accountability inside the business.
- Who may perform the action: Managers or administrators with authority to manage staff records.
- Retention expectations: Retain as part of employment history and governance records.

### System configuration changes
- What should be recorded: The configuration area changed, the previous state, the new state, the actor, and the purpose.
- Why it matters: Configuration changes can affect business behavior and must be reviewable.
- Who may perform the action: Authorized administrators with responsibility for system governance.
- Retention expectations: Retain for operational review and continuity planning.

## Audit expectations
Audit records should be clear enough for a business reviewer to understand what happened, why it happened, and whether the action followed the expected authority. Auditing should not be limited to failures; it should also preserve meaningful successful business actions where accountability matters.
