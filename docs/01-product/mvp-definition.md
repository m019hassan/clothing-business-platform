# MVP Definition

The MVP should be a practical first release for a small clothing business that wants to manage its daily operations more reliably. It should help the business run the core flow from product availability to order fulfillment without requiring a large team or excessive complexity.

## Why this MVP is realistic
The MVP focuses on the parts of the business that are most important for day-to-day operations: products, inventory, orders, payments, shipping, and basic administration. It avoids advanced manufacturing, analytics, and automation so the business can adopt it quickly and operate it consistently.

## Included MVP capabilities
- Product catalog management with variants
- Shared inventory across store and online channels
- Order creation and fulfillment tracking
- Cash on delivery and bank transfer payment handling with verification
- Shipping and delivery status handling
- Basic employee roles and permissions
- Notification support for key business events
- Simple reporting for core business activity

## Why each feature is included
- Product and inventory management are the foundation for sales and fulfillment.
- Orders and payments are essential because they directly affect customer trust and cash flow.
- Shipping and notifications are required to keep operations moving and customers informed.
- Role-based administration is necessary to reduce misuse and support accountability.
- Basic reporting is included so managers and owners can understand the business without relying on manual spreadsheets.

## What is intentionally excluded
- Advanced AI automation
- Advanced ERP-style manufacturing
- Complex analytics
- Large-scale multi-warehouse optimization
- Complex loyalty and promotion systems

## MVP success expectation
The MVP should be usable by a real small clothing business that wants to run sales, inventory, and fulfillment from one consistent process without overbuilding the first release.
